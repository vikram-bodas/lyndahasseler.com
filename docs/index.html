IBiography

A conductor who makes choirs tell a story

Lynda R. Hasseler, D.M.A., served as Professor of Music and Director of Choral Activities in the Conservatory of Music at Capital University in Columbus, Ohio, from 1990 until her retirement in 2026. When she assumed the directorship of the Chapel Choir in July 1990, she became the first woman to head a choral program at a Lutheran college or university.

Over thirty-five years she built a program recognized for artistic excellence, educational depth, and a national and international presence. Choirs under her direction performed for regional, national, and international conferences and festivals and toured across the United States and abroad. The Chapel Choir earned a gold medal at the 2012 World Choir Games in Cincinnati, and in 2019 gave the premiere of ACDA’s Brock Commission, a new work by Jake Runestad, at the American Choral Directors Association National Conference in Kansas City. She also led the university’s beloved Christmas Festival, which draws capacity audiences to Mees Hall every year.

Her programming drew on classic and contemporary a cappella literature, sacred and secular, chosen from a global perspective. Her choirs became known for diverse repertoire, thoughtful staging, and a range of tone colors suited to each style, and her narrative, theme-based concerts were built to tell a story.

On the podium

Dr. Hasseler travels widely as a clinician, guest conductor, and presenter. On Leap Day, February 29, 2016, she made her Carnegie Hall conducting debut in Leonard Bernstein’s Chichester Psalms and Ēriks Ešenvalds’ Trinity Te Deum. She has served on juries for international competitions, including the 14th Concorso Corale Internazionale in Riva del Garda, Italy, and the Sing Berlin! International Festival, and on the Music for All National Artistic Advisory Committee. With composer Craig Courtney, she has served as co-editor of the Capital University Choral Series, published by Beckenhorst Press.

As a singer

A mezzo-soprano, she has performed, toured, and recorded with the Robert Shaw Festival Chorus and the Oregon Bach Festival Chorus, singing in Carnegie Hall with both. She toured France with the Shaw Festival Chorus, appearing as a soloist, and toured internationally with the Oregon Bach Festival Choir, performing in Kraków and Warsaw, Santiago de Compostela, and Frankfurt and Stuttgart. She shares in the 2000 Grammy Award for Best Choral Performance, won by the Oregon Bach Festival Chorus and Orchestra under Helmuth Rilling for Krzysztof Penderecki’s Credo.

Beyond the music

Ask her about the richest moments life has to offer and she will name a fast-paced rehearsal, a well-crafted musical phrase, and the work of shaping choral tone with the “best students in the whole world.” Only one thing tops them all: being mom to Hannah, the “best daughter in the whole world.”

IIITeaching

In the rehearsal room

Dr. Hasseler built a culture of disciplined preparation, ensemble integrity, and meaningful storytelling through music. Her approach is rooted in a deep connection to text, sound, and expression, and it asks singers to bring their whole hearts to the work.

i.

Preparation

Fast-paced, focused rehearsals that build musicianship and leave room for artistry.

ii.

Tone and text

A range of tone colors shaped to each style of music, always in service of the words.

iii.

Story

Narrative, theme-based programs that carry an audience from the first note to the last.

Signature session

Out of the 4-Pattern and into the Heart

Teaching “open hearted singing” with rehearsal techniques that build beautiful choral tone, develop musicianship, encourage expressiveness, and nurture emotional intelligence and vulnerability.

ACDA Michigan Fall Conference, 2018
